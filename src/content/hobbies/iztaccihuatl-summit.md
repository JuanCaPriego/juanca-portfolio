Iztaccíhuatl (5,230 m) — "the sleeping woman" — as part of a mountaineering course I took, the opportunity to travel and attempt to summit this magnificent place presented to me and due to lack of preparation I couldn't make it.

## The approach

We started from Paso de Cortes since it snowed on previous days and the car's road was closed until the end of the weekend. Having to walk with camping equipment from Paso de Cortes adds up to 7 km to the already challanging route and that extra effort really caught up as we kept moving forward.

After making it to the third checkpoint, still many hours away from the glaciar, our professor decided it was best to set camp and rest to attempt the summit well rested.

One of the things that made it a really different experience from other camping places we had tested was the wind. Many considerations to place our tent in the best place we could find to avoid experiencing extreme wind flows or mud conditions which froze on the morning.

Since I was poorly prepared on this trip, I didn't have the energy to continue and had to stay in camp, from here I was able to see with less hurry beautiful landspaces and experience a relaxing morning at 4,700m above sea level.

## What I'd do differently

- Have a better diet along the week before the trip — preparing for the mountain is fundamental from das before, eating well, drinking a lot of water and electrolytes, not bring extra weight and train cardio.
- Challange me more - after some reflection, I think I could have gone farther up the mountain but wanted to play it extremely safe to not ruin the summit for the others but that made me stop before I was out of gas.

Outside of the technical notes, this climb is really about pacing — matching effort to elevation and being honest with yourself about turnaround time without being self condescending.
