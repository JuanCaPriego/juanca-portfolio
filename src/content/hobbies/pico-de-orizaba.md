Pico de Orizaba (Citlaltépetl) tops out at 5,636 m, making it to the glaciar. The standard Jamapa Glacier route is straightforward technically, but the altitude and glacier travel demand real
preparation.

## Logistics

We based out of the Piedra Grande hut (~4,260 m) and moved to the glacier in the dark, aiming to summit and be off the ice before the sun softened the snow.

## The climb

The lower slopes are loose scree — slow, grinding progress. Once on the glacier, crampons and consistent pace matter more than raw fitness. I had a lot of hesitation about this summit as I saw my performance in the Izztacihuatl summit push and gave myself a personal achievement to get. Reach the 5k meters above sea level mark. That was my goal.

One of the many advantages to start the push really early is that you have a lot of time in the dark and that helps the mental state. The only thing you can see is a small portion of the environment around you, one can stop the impulse of looking at how long until you reach the next checkpoint.

Getting to see snow in Mexico is not a common experience and watching the small details like the difference in density as you walk over the terrain, the variety of ice or the small fauna that live here is very enlightening.

Once I got to the beginning of the glaciar I was really amazed by yhe views, the peace and the effort required to get there, and all of that gave me an extra 10% of energy to experience a glaciar travel

## Takeaway

This one reinforced something I keep relearning: altitude doesn't care how fit you are
at sea level. Acclimatization days are not optional.
